## What they had

The Car Guys had just bought the domain. There was no site, no pages, and nothing anyone
could fill in.

The business does three different things and they are not versions of each other. You can
buy or lease a new car, sell the car you already have, or hand back a lease early, and all
of it happens without going to a dealership. Each one needs different information from a
different kind of person, and each one ends in an application that a human being has to
read.

They brought me in to design and build all of it, the site and the application. So it could
not be a brochure with a contact form at the bottom. It had to carry three services and take
real applications the day it went up.

## The waitlist

The real site was going to take a few more days and I did not want the domain sitting empty
while we built it, so I designed a coming soon page and put it up straight away.

It asks five things: first name, last name, email, phone, and a message. I put the message
box in because a name and an email says nothing about why somebody came. Someone who is
mid-lease and wants out will say so there, and that is a lead the team can do something
with. The three chips above it say what the business does, lease buyouts, auto brokering, no
dealer markup, so a visitor can tell whether they are in the right place before they type
anything.

## The wireframes

I wireframed every page in Figma first, in grey, with no photography and no brand colour.
That is because a dark hero with a car in it makes almost any layout look good, and I wanted
us arguing about the order of the sections and the length of the forms while both were still
cheap to change.

## The site

I built it in Webflow from those wireframes. Four pages: the home page and one for each
service.

Every service page is the same shape. A headline that says what you can do, one button, then
How It Works as three steps, then the form. Somebody who lands on Sell Your Car from a
search gets the same structure as somebody who came through the home page, so the site only
has to be learned once.

## The application

This is where most of the work went.

The form we were working from was an existing application from another company in the same
business, and it ran to 43 fields across three tabs. Eleven about the business, fifteen
about the personal guarantor, and seventeen more about that guarantor's employment, ending
in a signature. It had no branching, so everyone answered all of it. A sole trader with no
guarantor still filled in three tabs of guarantor questions, and a first tab that asks for a
Tax ID is a strange thing to show somebody who just wants to lease a car.

So I split it into two. The individual form asks about a person, the business form asks
about a business, and neither one shows the other's questions. Each sits on a single page
rather than being spread across tabs, so you can see how much is left.

The co-signer section moved behind a toggle, off by default. That is twenty one fields that
only appear for the people who actually have a co-signer, and it is most of the difference
between 43 and 22.

## Result

A business now fills 22 fields instead of 43, and a person fills 29 without ever seeing a
question about a guarantor they do not have. The site is live at thecarguysinc.com and
covers all three services, and the waitlist was up taking messages the whole time it was
being built.
