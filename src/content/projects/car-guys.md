## The problem

The Car Guys had just bought the domain. There was no site, no pages, and nothing anyone
could fill in. The business buys, leases and sells cars and takes over leases, all without
a dealership visit, so the site had to carry four different things a stranger might want to
do, and it had to be able to take real applications the day it went up.

## The waitlist, first

Rather than leave the domain empty while the real site was being designed, I built them a
coming soon page and put it up straight away. It asks five things: first name, last name,
email, phone, and a message. That gave them somewhere to send people and a list to talk to
before the site existed. Drawn at three widths, like everything that came after it.

## The site

Designed in Figma and built in Webflow. Four page types, the home page and one for each
service, each drawn for desktop and for phones. Every service page carries the same three
step How It Works, so someone who lands on Sell Your Car reads the same shape as someone
who lands on the home page.

<img src="/images/projects/car-guys-3.avif" alt="The three service pages, each with the same three step How It Works" width="720" height="450" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Responsive the whole way down, not just below a breakpoint. The navigation collapses to a
menu at tablet width and the three step rows stack on a phone.

<img src="/images/projects/car-guys-4.avif" alt="The home page at desktop, tablet and phone width" width="720" height="450" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

## The application

The form we were working from, an existing application from another company in the same
business, ran to 43 fields across three tabs: eleven about the business, fifteen about the
personal guarantor, and seventeen more about that guarantor's employment. Everyone answered
all of it, whether they had a guarantor or not.

I split it into two, one for a person and one for a business, put each on a single page
instead of three tabs, and moved the co-signer behind a toggle so it only appears for
people who have one.

<img src="/images/projects/car-guys-5.avif" alt="The application on one page, with the co-signer section folded away behind its toggle" width="720" height="450" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

## Result

A business now fills 22 fields instead of 43. The site is live and covers all three
services, and the waitlist was up taking messages the whole time it was being built.
