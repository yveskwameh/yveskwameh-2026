## What they had

BOH Financial does accounting for creators, entertainers and the businesses around them, and
they sell it as a subscription rather than by the hour.

That is a real difference from an ordinary accounting firm and the old site was not carrying
it. It was a services list, and a services list from an accounting firm reads much the same
whoever wrote it.

There was a second thing underneath. They sell to three groups who want different things:
artists and performers, the agencies and managers who represent them, and founders running a
small business. A single page has to serve all three at once, so it ends up general, which is
the one thing they were trying not to be.

They brought me in to design and build the site.

## The home page

The home page does one job. A headline that says who the firm is for, three cards for the
three audiences, and a login for people who are already clients. That is the whole page.

I kept it that short deliberately. Someone arriving already knows whether they are an artist,
an agency or a business owner, so the most useful thing the page can do is let them say which
and then get out of the way. The navigation is the same idea, the only content menu on it is
Who We Help.

## A page each

Each card opens a page written for that audience alone. The talent page leads with "For
Artists, Musicians, Influencers, And Performers Managing Their Business", and it carries the
same photograph as the card that was clicked, so you can see you landed where you aimed.

That is what going multi-page bought us. The talent page can talk about royalties and touring
without a restaurant owner reading past it, and the small business page can do the reverse.

## Ten services, folded

Talent alone has ten services: bookkeeping, contract and royalty administration, tax
compliance, payroll, cash flow, insurance, bill pay, wealth coordination, entity structuring,
and advisory. Every one of them needs a paragraph to explain properly.

So I folded them. Closed, each one is a single line, and the ten together answer what do you
actually do in the space of a screen. Open, one at a time, each gives its detail to the
person who wants that one thing. Royalty administration matters enormously to a musician and
not at all to a restaurant, and neither of them should have to scroll past the other's answer
to get to their own.

## Testimonials and pricing

The testimonials sit under the services rather than above them, because a quote is worth more
once you know what is being sold. They are attributed by name and role, a musician, a
production company, an actor, so a visitor can find the one closest to themselves.

<img src="/images/projects/boh-5@720.avif" alt="Testimonials placed after the services, attributed by role" width="720" height="322" loading="lazy" decoding="async" srcset="/images/projects/boh-5@720.avif 720w, /images/projects/boh-5@1440.avif 1440w, /images/projects/boh-5@2880.avif 2880w" sizes="(max-width: 640px) 100vw, min(100vw, 1440px)" />

Then the prices, in the open. Three tiers for talent, named for where an artist is in their
career rather than Basic, Pro and Premium, and each one includes everything in the tier below
it.

Publishing them was the right call here because BOH sell a monthly subscription. Putting a
monthly price behind an enquiry form would have argued against the thing the rest of the page
was saying.

## The whole page

The home page end to end at the size it was built. It is short, and the shortness is the
design.

## Result

The firm now has a page for each of the three groups it sells to, in that group's language,
with its own services, its own proof and its own published prices. A musician and a
restaurant owner never see each other's page. For a firm whose whole pitch is that they
understand creators, that is what the old site was not doing.
