## What they had

Altabrio were weeks from launching a training product, which is a bad moment to find out the
navigation does not hold. Everything was built. What it needed was not more features, it was
to make sense to somebody opening it for the first time.

A training product has a second difficulty. It produces a lot of data, and most of it means
nothing on its own. A resting heart rate is just a number until it sits next to what it was
last week.

They brought me in to sort the navigation out and rebuild the identity, with the launch date
staying where it was.

## The way in

The first thing anyone meets is the sign in, so it does the introducing. What the product is
sits beside the form rather than on a page before it, so you do not have to read a pitch to
reach your account, and if you arrived from an ad you still learn what you signed up for.

## Setting up is part of the product

An adaptive training plan is worthless without the athlete's own numbers in it, so the setup
asks for them. I made it a sequence of short steps with a progress bar rather than one long
form, because somebody who has just signed up has not yet decided this is worth twenty
minutes.

## The dashboard answers one question

The layout puts the few numbers that answer "how am I doing" in front, grouped rather than
spread, and keeps everything else one move away instead of competing on the same screen. The
greeting and the date are there because a training app gets opened daily, so the useful frame
is today rather than all time.

## One system, three more screens

The rest of the product is the calendar, the races and the vitals, and all three are the same
components rearranged rather than three designs. A calendar of sessions, a table of races, a
table of vitals: one sidebar, one header, one set of table styles, one way a row behaves.

That is what made the deadline survivable. Once the system was settled a new screen was an
arrangement rather than a design, so the remaining weeks went on the screens that still
needed thinking about.

## Result

Navigation untangled, the identity rebuilt, and a product that went out on the date they
already had, understandable to the people who signed up first.
