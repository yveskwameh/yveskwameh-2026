## The problem

Altabrio was weeks from launching a training product, which is the worst moment to discover
that the navigation does not hold. Everything was built. What it needed was not more
features, it was to be legible to somebody opening it for the first time.

A training product also has a particular problem: it generates a great deal of data and
almost none of it is interesting on its own. A number is only useful next to the thing it
is supposed to be, and a screen that shows forty numbers with equal weight is a screen
nobody reads twice.

## The way in

The first thing anyone meets is the sign in, so it does the introducing. What the product
is sits beside the form rather than on a page before it, so nobody has to read a pitch to
reach their account, and somebody arriving from an ad still learns what they signed up for.

## Setting up is part of the product

An adaptive training plan is worthless without the athlete's own numbers in it, so the
setup asks for them. It is a sequence of short steps with a progress bar rather than one
long form, because someone who has just signed up has not yet decided this is worth
twenty minutes.

## The dashboard answers one question

The layout puts the few numbers that answer "how am I doing" in front, grouped rather than
spread, and keeps everything else one move away instead of on the same screen competing.
The greeting and the date are there because a training app is opened daily and the useful
frame is today, not all time.

## The same system, four ways

The rest of the product is calendar, races and plans, and all three are the same components
rearranged rather than three designs. A calendar of sessions, a table of races, a table of
plans: one sidebar, one header, one set of table styles, one way a row behaves.

That is what made the deadline survivable. Once the system was settled, a new screen was an
arrangement rather than a design, and the remaining weeks went on the screens that still
needed thinking about.

## Result

Navigation streamlined, the visual identity rebuilt, and a product that could go out on
time and be understood by the people who signed up first.
